Keep the magic. Replace the obligation.

VocalRender’s opening vocal identity is worth protecting, but it should not be forced to become an entire Suno competitor. Build a model-agnostic music workstation around it, then let controlled bake-offs decide each engine.

Its best two to three seconds may be the project’s only demonstrated differentiated asset. Isolate and test it before discarding it.

Where the project really is

The prototype has crossed an important threshold—one short passage sounds unusually convincing—but it has not yet crossed the product threshold of stable identity, correct lyrics, full-song structure, and repeatable editing.

What the two-second result means

It is evidence that the conditioning can capture the desired timbre. It is not evidence that the model can sustain it. The next experiment should repeatedly re-inject the same reference every 2.5, 3, and 4 seconds, with several overlaps/crossfades, then score the head, middle, tail, seams, lyrics, and melody separately. A similar long-form drift pattern has been reported in VoxCPM, but its proposed chunking workaround is not yet proven.2 3

Product boundary

The Amy-like path is a research benchmark, not the product voice. Current local data terms and voice-rights constraints require a consented/original voice catalog, provenance records, revocation, and explicit disclosure before commercialization.28 29

The target moved: Suno is now a studio

Competing on waveform quality alone is no longer enough. Suno now combines v5.5 generation with owned-voice features, section editing, stems, MIDI prompting, effects, automation, and multitrack export.23 24

Audio bar

A coherent, finished song

Stable structure, correct lyrics, expressive lead vocal, genre-faithful arrangement, and a mix that survives headphones and speakers.

Workflow bar

A revision-friendly workstation

Regenerate one section, swap a singer, preserve the rest, expose stems, import melody/MIDI, compare variations, and export to a DAW.

Why there is no honest “best model” winner yet

ACE-Step, HeartMuLa, LeVo2, and newer research systems use different prompts, commercial model versions, raters, and metrics. Some papers reverse each other’s rankings. Automated audio judges also have measurable bias, so same-prompt blind listening is the deciding experiment.25 26

What others did in the same situation

The repeated lesson is not “find one bigger checkpoint.” Teams split the problem where quality fails, then make those parts independently replaceable.

Pattern 1

Plan globally, render locally

ACE-Step, MiniMax Music3, HeartMuLa, LeVo2, and Alibaba’s frontier system use separate planning/semantic and acoustic/rendering stages. This keeps song-level intent from depending on one long raw-audio trajectory.5 6 27

Pattern 2

Split vocals from accompaniment

LeVo2 refines vocal and accompaniment streams separately; AccompGen builds accompaniment around an isolated vocal. That is a direct match for VocalRender’s narrow but potentially valuable vocal strength.10 22

Pattern 3

Generate performance, then convert identity

SoulX-Singer, Seed-VC, and Vevo2 treat singer identity/style as a specialist audio-to-audio stage. This avoids asking the composition model to also be the best voice-cloner.13 14 15

Pattern 4

Re-anchor short windows when self-conditioning drifts

Practitioners confronting VoxCPM’s long-form drift proposed splitting audio and reinjecting the original prompt. VocalRender’s failure starts at a similar boundary; this makes chunking the next controlled test, not a guaranteed solution.3

Pattern 5

Turn generation into an editable studio

Suno’s product moved into stems, section replacement, MIDI, effects, automation, and multitrack export. The workflow remains useful even as the underlying model changes.23 24

Pattern to avoid

Trusting one vendor leaderboard

Model papers use incompatible prompts, raters, and commercial baselines; automated judges introduce their own bias. Strong teams freeze prompts and listen blind to complete songs and time slices.25 26

Recommended architecture

Treat models as replaceable engines. The durable product is the song specification, evaluation harness, editing workflow, consented voice system, and the ability to route each task to the best renderer.

The product moat

Not one checkpoint: a reproducible song manifest, controllable edits, consistent consented voices, automatic candidate ranking, and a listener-tested workflow that survives model churn.

The model moat

If the re-anchored VocalRender experiment works, the distinctive asset is a score-native identity renderer that can replace only selected phrases without regenerating the song.

Execution plan

The sequence is designed to answer “is the voice magic durable?” and “which backbone is actually best?” before expensive architecture work.

Days 1–3

Freeze the test

  • Six song briefs covering ballad, pop, rock, soul, country, and dense rap.
  • Three consented/original voice references.
  • Save lyrics, MIDI/score, references, seeds, stems, runtimes, and failures in one song manifest.
Days 3–7

Run the decisive experiments

  • VocalRender chunks: 2.5/3/4 s × 100/200/400 ms overlaps.
  • ACE-Step 1.5 2B on the 4070; try XL with offload only after 2B works.
  • Same briefs through Lyria 3 Pro and Eleven Music v2.
Weeks 2–5

Build the router + studio spine

  • One adapter interface for local/API engines.
  • Stem ingestion, waveform/section timeline, regenerate-one-section, A/B take comping.
  • SoulX, Seed-VC, and Vevo2 vocal bake-off over the best source vocal.
Weeks 6–12

Converge on a product

  • Restore 3090 visibility and test MiniMax Music3.
  • Choose one default and one fallback engine from measured results.
  • Add consent, provenance, disclosure, revocation, exports, and a closed alpha.

Backbone go/no-go gate

  • At least 90% of three-minute runs finish without structural collapse.
  • Median English WER ≤15% on the frozen suite.
  • At least 40% blind preference against the stronger API baseline, with ties allowed.
  • No single dimension—vocal, melody, structure, arrangement, mix, musicality—scores below 3/5 median.

Voice-layer go/no-go gate

  • Head, middle, and tail identity differ by ≤0.5 on a five-point listener scale.
  • Audible seam rate below 10% of transitions.
  • WER worsens by no more than five absolute points versus the source vocal.
  • Every product voice has explicit consent, provenance, and a revocation path.

These are proposed engineering gates, not claims that any current model meets them. SongBench’s seven dimensions are a useful rubric; listener preference remains primary.25

Risk controls that change the design

Do not train on Suno output

Current Suno terms bar using the service or outputs to build or train a competing service. Use independently licensed, public-domain, commissioned, or consented material instead.24

Keep APIs behind adapters

Lyria is preview-only; Eleven adds pure-play co-branding and usage constraints. A provider outage or terms change must not invalidate saved projects.

Human authorship must be visible

Song manifests, lyric/section/MIDI edits, take selection, and detailed transformations are useful product features and evidence of human creative control. Copyrightability remains fact-specific.28

Judge the whole song in time slices

Measure opening, middle, and tail separately. The current aggregate judge missed the most important failure: a great opening followed by a collapsing identity.
