A six-lens investigation into prompt-conditioned singing voice synthesis: how much synthetic data is worth building, whether zero-shot timbre can reach a held-out artist, what a Chinese→English finetune actually requires, and which English singing corpora exist. Every load-bearing claim traced to a primary source.

01

The 60-Second Summary

An English LoRA that moved WER 44.7 → 29.8 % while moving voice identity by exactly zero is not a bug report — it is the architecture working as designed. In prompt-conditioned systems, timbre enters as a conditioning input from the reference clip, and MiniMax-Speech states outright that it uses LoRA for emotion control and not for timbre; UtterTune independently reports speaker similarity pinned at ~0.69 across every system variant while accent correctness moved 0.472 → 0.975. The 0.922 SIM that makes VocalRender look like a solved timbre problem is a saturated, confounded, out-of-domain metric: it sits inside the paper’s own ground-truth range of 0.918–0.929, it is computed by a WavLM-TDNN encoder trained on speech that scores roughly 40 % EER on VocalSet’s extended vocal techniques, and the prompt is cut from the same song as the target so mix and room cues inflate it. The contested part is what to do instead: the AI-cover community reaches convincing artist timbre with 10–30 minutes of clean vocal because RVC retains a FAISS index of the artist’s actual feature vectors rather than compressing identity into one embedding — but a Seed-VC self-evaluation reports zero-shot beating per-speaker RVC on cosine similarity while losing on audio quality, so “conversion beats zero-shot” is not settled, only “conversion wins on the axes humans grade.” The real tension is that both remaining halves of the project are bottlenecked on things the user has been treating as solved: English quality is a phoneme-representation problem that five independent systems fixed with shared IPA rather than more hours, and artist grit is subharmonic period-doubling that every singing vocoder’s periodic-plus-aperiodic assumption structurally cannot represent.

02

Six Key Findings, Ranked by Reliability

VocalRender’s SIM 0.922 is a saturated metric with no headroom, measured by a speech encoder that is near-chance on singing technique
1
Reliability: High
9/10

Three independently verified facts stack. First, the paper’s own ground-truth topline is 0.918–0.929 — the reported 0.922 for VocalRender and 0.929 for Pro equal or exceed real recordings, so the metric cannot distinguish good from perfect. Second, singing SIM is field-standard computed with WavLM-TDNN or ECAPA trained on VoxCeleb/CN-Celeb, i.e. speech only; on VocalSet, which is specifically extended vocal technique, all speech-trained speaker-verification baselines score ~40 % EER — near chance. Third, VocalRender’s prompt is a random 2–8 s segment from the same song as the target, and ASV embeddings are known to be biased by channel confounders such as noise and EQ. Capped at 9 rather than 10 because the step from “the encoder is bad at singing” to “the encoder is blind to rasp specifically” is inference: no study probes voice quality in speaker embeddings.

2
Reliability: High
9/10

MiniMax-Speech describes a learnable speaker encoder that takes untranscribed reference audio and produces a fixed-size vector capturing “pure vocal identity, disentangled from semantic content,” and is explicit that LoRA is used for emotion control, not timbre. UtterTune provides the controlled corroboration: LoRA r=16 on CosyVoice2 moved Japanese accent correctness 0.472 → 0.975 over 20k steps while speaker similarity stayed at ~0.69 across all systems. The contrast case makes it coherent — in ACE-Step, which has no speaker-prompt encoder, practitioners report DiT LoRA does teach vocal timbre from 20–50 tracks. The difference is architectural, and it is exactly the difference that predicts a null result on a prompt-conditioned model.

Cross-lingual failure here is a phoneme-representation problem, not a data-volume problem
3
Reliability: Medium-high
8/10

Five independent systems converge. DiaMoE-TTS extends an F5-TTS Chinese+English checkpoint to new dialects via a unified IPA lexicon. Transinger — the only cross-lingual SVS result found — covers Korean, English, Chinese and Japanese on 40.9 h total by decomposing IPA phonemes into letters plus diacritics so pronunciation generalises compositionally to unseen languages. BiSinger manufactures bilingual training data over a shared CMU-dict phone space. XTTS sidesteps the clash entirely by romanising CJK. And GPT-SoVITS is the cautionary case: Chinese receives BERT embeddings while English is zero-padded, and users report mispronunciations surviving both further finetuning and DPO. Adding hours against a mismatched frontend repeatedly fails; changing the phoneme representation repeatedly works. Scored 8 rather than 9 because this is convergence across differing architectures, not a single controlled ablation isolating representation from volume.

Vocal grit is subharmonic period-doubling — a phenomenon the periodic-plus-aperiodic model cannot represent
4
Reliability: Medium-high
8/10

Peer-reviewed voice science establishes that rough voice is carried by subharmonics — energy peaks between consecutive harmonics, i.e. period-doubling — and critically that voices can be perceptually rough with entirely normal jitter and shimmer. Growl involves simultaneous vocal-fold and supraglottal sub-harmonic co-oscillation. Meanwhile every standard singing vocoder decomposes the waveform into a periodic component plus an aperiodic component; Period Singer needed separate CVAEs for the two to fix naturalness. Subharmonic phonation is neither: it is periodic at half or a third of F0. Separately, analysis of Stable Audio Open’s VAE shows reconstruction “less faithful in tracking the ground-truth spectrum, most visibly in the high-frequency region” — and engineers locate rasp energy at ~2.5 kHz with harmonic partners at 5 and 10 kHz, the 10 kHz partner being what compression kills first. Score capped at 8: each premise is solidly sourced, but the conclusion that grit dies in VocalRender’s VAE specifically is an untested hypothesis — see the frontier question.

Challenged byNo one directly — but nobody has measured subharmonic survival through a codec either
Model collapse does not threaten a synthetic-pretrain schedule — but cherry-picking outputs does
5
Reliability: Medium-high
8/10

Shumailov et al. (Nature 631:755–759, 2024) is the canonical collapse result, and its protocol replaces real data at each generation. Gerstgrasser et al. prove that when synthetic data accumulates alongside real data, test error carries a finite upper bound independent of iteration count — demonstrated for language models, VAEs on images, and diffusion. A synthetic-pretrain-then-real-finetune schedule is accumulate-style, so the collapse framing largely does not apply. The live risk is different: Alemohammad et al. (ICLR 2024) show that sampling bias — keeping only the outputs that sound good — trades diversity for realism and degrades within a few generations. Corrected during verification: an earlier framing treated collapse as the governing risk; the governing rule is accumulate-not-replace, plus do-not-cherry-pick.

Contested signal · monitor, do not assert · confidence 5/10

“Per-artist voice conversion beats zero-shot prompting for identity”

The architectural argument is strong: RVC stores all training-set HuBERT/ContentVec 768-d vectors in a FAISS index and retrieves the top-8 nearest target vectors at inference, so identity is retrieved rather than summarised into one embedding — and the academic literature agrees a fixed-size embedding “does not suffice to capture how the target speaker performs various singing techniques.” But the Seed-VC repository’s own evaluation reports zero-shot conversion from a single random utterance beating per-character RVCv2 on SECS and CER, while losing on DNSMOS audio quality. That is an author-run, objective-metric-only comparison, and SECS is a cosine metric subject to every criticism in Finding 1. Do not state that conversion beats zero-shot. The defensible reconciliation: zero-shot can match on embedding-space similarity while losing on audio quality and style dynamics — which are precisely what a human or LLM judge grades, and why nothing in the user’s tests cleared 5/10.

Contested signal · monitor, do not assert · confidence 4/10

“X hours are required to adapt to a new language”

Five sources, five irreconcilable answers. VoxCPM’s own docs say 500+ h. F5-TTS practitioners say 100–300 h. A documented Vietnamese run with 60 h and 160k steps produced unintelligible output. Phir Hera Fairy reports 10 h per language retaining emergent behaviour at ~0.8 % average metric loss. Muskits adapted KiSing on 42.6 minutes. These differ in architecture, in whether the target language was already in pretraining, and in step count — and step count is a hidden variable no source isolates. The honest answer to “how many hours” is that the field does not know, and that steps and phoneme representation may matter more than hours.

03

The Hidden Connection

Two findings look unrelated. Finding 1 says the field’s timbre metric is saturated and measured by the wrong encoder. Finding 4 says vocal grit lives in subharmonics that vocoders structurally cannot represent. Held apart, one is a measurement complaint and the other is a signal-processing complaint.

They are the same fact seen from two ends of the pipeline. The speaker-verification encoder scores ~40 % EER on VocalSet because extended vocal technique — growl, rasp, vocal fry — is subharmonic content that a model trained on clean read speech never had to represent. The vocoder loses that content for the same structural reason: its periodic-plus-aperiodic decomposition has no slot for period-doubling. So the generator throws away exactly the information the evaluator cannot see is missing. This is why SVCC 2025 could find five systems statistically indistinguishable from ground truth on identity while reproducing dynamic vocal technique at only 37–44 % accuracy, and why SVCC 2023 saw naturalness reach human level while not one team of 26 matched target-speaker similarity. The field optimised the number it could compute.

A blind evaluator and a deaf generator, failing on the same physics — which means the 8.0-versus-3.0 gap on a human-anchored judge is not noise around a good metric, it is the signal the good-looking metric was never able to carry.

The assumption this briefing rests on (and the missing seventh lens)

All six branches froze one variable: that this architecture is the vehicle. Every finding about prompts, LoRA rank, phoneme sets and corpora is downstream of accepting a prompt-conditioned latent-diffusion SVS as the thing being improved. A branch that started from “what produces convincing artist vocals in 2026, by any means” would have foregrounded the hybrid generate-then-convert pipeline much earlier, and would have treated VocalRender as a performance generator whose timbre output is disposable.

The missing seventh lens is the rights-holder/estate — not as a legal risk, which Branch 6 covered, but as an evidentiary gap. The most capable artist-voice work is plausibly commercial and unpublished precisely because publishing it invites the exposure documented in Lehrman v. Lovo and the ELVIS Act. That means the literature’s silence on held-out-artist evaluation may reflect selection, not difficulty, and every “nobody has done this” finding in this report should be read as “nobody has published this.” If a capable closed system exists, the report’s central pessimism about zero-shot artist timbre could invert.

04

The Four Questions, Answered

Q1 — How much synthetic data, and does it cross languages?

Diminishing returns: unknown for singing. No SVS scaling curve exists in the synthetic regime. The only SVS data curve, DiTSinger, runs 30 h → 530 h with no saturation observed. The nearest knees come from ASR: 200–300 h in code-switching ASR (100→200 h gives 23 % relative WER gain, then 10.3 %, 4.1 %, 2.5 %), and ~20 h in small-scale accented-ASR finetuning beyond which synthetic data actively degrades. Neither is safe to port.

Cross-lingual transfer: one datapoint, positive, small. ACE-Opencpop’s synthetic Mandarin pretraining transferred to Japanese Kiritan for +0.10 MOS. That is the entire published evidence base for synthetic singing crossing a language boundary. Mandarin-synthetic → English-singing has never been published.

The most useful number for a 12 GB budget: a 1.7B TTS-pretrained speech LM finetuned on 135 h of synthetic singing matched SOTA discrete SVS (SingMOS 4.09 vs 4.08) — but F0 RMSE lagged 62.79 vs 55.83 Hz. Consistent with the cross-cutting pattern that the synthetic-data penalty in SVS concentrates in pitch, not intelligibility. Two further constraints: synthetic-only training fails while mixing real recovers most of the loss, and in an adjacent task a curated 700 h subset beat a full 2,500 h set — filtering beats growing.

Q2 — Has anyone reached a held-out artist, and what SIM is convincing?

No, and there is no such SIM value. Across SVCC 2023, SVCC 2025, SoulX-Singer-Eval, TCSinger, StyleSinger and VocalRender, every “unseen singer” is a recruited amateur, a corpus speaker, or a Mixing Secrets multitrack. Not one paper evaluates a named, commercially distinctive recording artist as a held-out target with human similarity ratings.

Nor is there a published cosine threshold for human-convincing timbre. VoxSim gives ASV cosine-to-human correlation of ~0.75 in-domain but ~0.50 on out-of-domain synthetic speech — the correlation collapses precisely where synthesis lives, and that is speech, not singing. SVCC reports Spearman ρ ≈ −0.63 (2023) and ≈ 0.8 (2025) for singer-embedding distance, with organisers stating objective metrics are “not at a level where [they] could be considered a true replacement for subjective scores.” The commonly cited SECS ≥ 0.80 “same speaker” rule is encoder-specific convention with no perceptual validation.

Prompt length is not a lever. VALL-E 2 and Voicebox both report SIM growing quickly then flattening — ~3 s captures most timbre, with 3/5/10 s roughly equivalent and only 1 s failing. The user’s 8.6 s test moving nothing is exactly what the literature predicts. There is no published information-theoretic bound on prompt-carried identity; only this empirical saturation curve.

Q3 — Is there a Chinese→English finetune recipe?

Not for singing. Transinger trains multilingually from scratch; DiaMoE-TTS stays inside Chinese. Adapting an already-Mandarin-finetuned SVS checkpoint to English is unpublished territory. What exists is transferable guidance from TTS:

Q4 — What English corpora beat GTSinger?

None, on the combination that matters. Full inventory follows in section 05. The short answer: for licence, SingStyle111 (CC BY 4.0) is a clear upgrade at half the hours; for volume, DSing30 (149.1 h) exists but has no note annotation and inherits a non-commercial Smule licence; for annotation breadth, DALI v2 has 7,756 note-level English-dominant songs but distributes no audio. Building an English analogue of ACE-Opencpop — a synthesised, note-aligned corpus at ~130 h — is the gap nobody has filled.

Separation also does nothing for licensing: separating a commercial track produces a derivative of a copyrighted recording regardless of dataset tagging.

07

What To Actually Do Differently

Ordered by evidence strength per unit of GPU time. The first three are cheap and settle questions currently being guessed at.

01
Stop treating voice identity as a training target on this architecture.

Finding 2 says a prompt-conditioned model cannot learn timbre into its weights — MiniMax uses LoRA for emotion explicitly not timbre, and UtterTune’s speaker similarity did not move across any variant. Every future LoRA should be evaluated on WER and melody accuracy only. Identity belongs to a separate stage.

02
Fix the unsaved-parameter bug at both sites, not one.

svs_utils.py:250 installs a fresh nn.Embedding that arrives requires_grad=True after freezing — the 151M phantom trainable params. svs_utils.py:115–130 rebuilds score_lm_head as a new nn.Linear with the same defect, which the handoff had not recorded. Either freeze both explicitly after resize, or widen the save filter beyond "lora_". Note the initialisation itself is fine — pitch, BPM and duration tokens get sinusoidal init, which is better than the mean-of-subtokens fix the literature recommends, so the “random init causes gibberish” theory is dead.

03
DONE — the subharmonic VAE round-trip measurement was run, and it changes the plan.

scripts_amy/vae_roundtrip_shr.py, four Amy stems, 4,421 voiced frames. Median SHR loss −10.9 %: grit largely survives the VAE, so the failure is in generation, not representation. Do not abandon in-model timbre work as physically impossible — that argument is closed off. Two operational consequences: the >8 kHz band of every render is decoder invention (the encoder is 16 kHz), so prompt clips carry no real information up there either; and post-VAE conversion remains the pragmatic path, but because the generator is weak, not because the latent is incapable.

04
Re-run the English LoRA at r=32, 20k steps, all four attention projections, with 10–20 % Chinese data mixed in.

Current run was r=16 at 1,000 steps. UtterTune moved accent correctness 0.472 → 0.975 at 20k steps in under an hour on a 4090. Kwon et al. tabulate r=32 beating r=16 on CER and WER, at some UTMOS cost. VoxCPM recommends the data mixing against forgetting. Expect WER to improve; expect identity not to move, and do not read that as failure.

05
Attack English through the phoneme frontend before buying more hours.

Five independent systems fixed cross-lingual quality by changing representation, not volume — unified IPA (DiaMoE-TTS), IPA decomposed into letters plus diacritics (Transinger, 4 languages on 40.9 h total), a shared CMU-dict phone space (BiSinger), or romanisation (XTTS). GPT-SoVITS is the counter-example where an asymmetric frontend made the problem unfixable by acoustic finetuning. Check what VocalRender’s text frontend actually does with English before adding data.

06
Move melody accuracy to a first-class metric now.

The synthetic-data penalty in SVS concentrates in pitch: the 135 h speech-LM result matched SOTA on SingMOS while trailing 7 Hz on F0 RMSE, and VocalRender itself documents a synthetic-versus-real pitch distribution mismatch. The paper’s own RPA is 0.72. The already-written eval_melody.py is the correct instrument and has never been run — and if a synthetic English corpus gets built, melodic-range and vibrato coverage must be checked, not just WER.

07
If artist timbre remains a goal, restructure as generate-then-convert, with conversion last.

The AI-cover community reaches convincing artist timbre from 10–30 min of clean isolated vocal at 250–500 epochs with RMVPE, because RVC retrieves from a FAISS index of the artist’s actual feature vectors rather than compressing identity into one embedding. The hybrid pipeline is standard practice, and the documented failure mode is running conversion before a generative stage, which carries artifacts through. Caveat honestly: the Seed-VC self-evaluation contests the ordering, so treat this as the best-supported hypothesis rather than a settled result. Note 0.4 h is above every published few-shot threshold — cleanliness of separated stems, not duration, is the risk.

08
Keep artifacts local. Publishing is the act that creates exposure.

Private, non-commercial experimentation sits outside EU AI Act scope by explicit Commission FAQ, and Tennessee has a scholarship exemption. But Lehrman v. Lovo established that copyright and Lanham claims fail while state right-of-publicity survives, the ELVIS Act reaches distribution of a tool whose primary purpose is one identifiable person’s voice, and NO FAKES — advanced from Senate Judiciary 18 Jun 2026, not yet law — has no general personal-use carve-out. Publishing an adapter trained on CC BY-NC-SA data simultaneously triggers the untested ELVIS tool prong and the unresolved ShareAlike-reaches-weights question.

✓ Safe — verified against primary source

  • VocalRender’s SIM 0.922 sits inside its own ground-truth band of 0.918–0.929 — the metric is saturated.

The Frontier Question

Does subharmonic energy — the actual acoustic carrier of rasp, growl and grit — survive a neural audio VAE round-trip?

Everything downstream depended on the answer and nobody had measured it. The voice-science literature establishes that roughness is period-doubling, distinct from jitter and shimmer. The vocoder literature establishes that singing synthesis models the waveform as periodic plus aperiodic, a decomposition with no slot for a subharmonic. The audio-VAE literature establishes that high-frequency spectral fidelity degrades in reconstruction. Three solid premises, never joined by an experiment — because the question falls between three literatures that do not cite each other.

Answered on 2026-08-09, after this report was drafted. Four Amy stems, 4,421 voiced frames, encode→decode with no generative step, subharmonic-to-harmonic ratio measured against an F0 track taken from the original. Median SHR loss: −10.9 % (range −1.1 % to −14.4 %). Real, but nowhere near the disappearance the three premises jointly predicted. The hypothesis is refuted: the subharmonic carrier of rasp largely survives the representation, and the VAE is not the primary reason renders score 3/10 against real Amy’s 8/10. By elimination the loss is in generation — the conditional-mean-seeking sampling path the over-smoothing literature describes, consistent with SVCC 2025 finding vocal technique at 37–44 % while identity looked solved.

One structural fact surfaced before any measurement and outlasts it: the VAE is asymmetric — it encodes at 16 kHz and decodes at 48 kHz (audio_vae_v2.py:439–440). Everything above the 8 kHz encode-side Nyquist is absent from the latent by construction, so the ~10 kHz rasp partner cannot survive at any decoder quality, and everything above 8 kHz in the output is decoder invention. Measured band deltas match exactly: the 2–3 kHz and 4.5–5.5 kHz bands are preserved to within half a dB, while 9–11 kHz and 12–16 kHz lose 1.5–6.9 dB. Full write-up in experiment-01-vae-roundtrip.md.
